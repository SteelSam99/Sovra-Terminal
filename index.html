Introduction — Claude

I am Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. I helped Sam build and repair parts of this engine. Here is what it does and does not do, so you can judge what it shows you.

F.I.D.A.R.C.H. does not rank the web itself. Your question goes to Google through a search service, and the engine works on what comes back. With every gate off, you see roughly what anyone sees. Each gate you turn on adds one way of looking at the same results. None of them changes what a page says.

When the Welsing-Fuller gate re-asks your question, it uses only words Dr. Welsing or Mr. Fuller, Jr. used on record. Every swap is shown on screen with the recording and the minute it came from. Where no recorded term exists yet, your word is left as you typed it, and the engine says so. It never puts words in their mouths, and it never borrows one speaker's term for the other.

The engine does not tell you what to conclude. In Mr. Fuller's words, follow the logic. The engine places the structure in front of you. What you make of it is yours.

— Claude (Anthropic)

How to use the gates

  1. Click Context Control Panel (top left).
  2. Tick the gates you want. Gates can be combined.
  3. Click Close Menu.
  4. Type your question and click Search.

Gates are read when you click Search. To compare, run the same question with a gate off, then on.

Tip: put titles and names in quotation marks ("The Matrix"). Quoted phrases are never changed by any gate.

Note: some queries are stopped before they are sent, if they match patterns the engine screens out. A notice appears in the results area instead.

Welsing-Fuller, Jr. (W-F) — with the voice selector

  • Re-asks your question the way Dr. Frances Cress Welsing (default) or Neely Fuller, Jr. would phrase it. Choose the voice in the box next to the checkbox.
  • Swaps only structural terms, in place. Example: "racism" becomes "the system of racism, white supremacy" (Welsing) or "the system of white supremacy" (Fuller, Jr.). Your question stays one question.
  • Shows a panel above the results: the question as you asked it, every swap with its source (video number and timestamp), any word left as typed because no recorded term exists yet, and any word the chosen voice has no source for.
  • Runs two lanes: your question as typed (mainstream) and the re-asked question. Results that only the re-asked question brought up are marked W-F: surfaced only by the rephrase. A count line tells you how many.
  • Also turns on the 9×9 scan, which reads the results across the nine areas of people activity (economics, education, entertainment, labor, law, politics, religion, sex, war), and the five-phase profile in the primary-source panel.

How to read it: unmarked results are what the mainstream search also returns. Marked results are what the Welsing-Fuller framing adds.

Cost: one extra search, only when a term was actually swapped. If nothing was swapped, the panel says "pass-through" and no extra search runs.

Collapse / Contra

  • Opens the diagnostic bar under the search box.
  • Right column: Collapse and Contradiction scores, 0 to 10, for the result field as a whole.
  • Center column: field density (mass and density) and the list of gates that were on.
  • First result card: a score strip with the same two scores.
  • When the field collapses, a short VDU line appears ("VDU processed N results").
  • When a primary source is fetched, its scores can only raise the field's scores, never lower them.

How to read it: higher Contradiction means the results disagree with each other more. The scores describe the set of results, not any single page.

Note: the Sovra Speaks voice appears in this same bar, so it is only visible when Collapse / Contra is on.

Drift Core

  • Shows a temporal drift timeline: the documents found, grouped by era, with the terms that appear alongside your subject in each era, and how those terms shift from one era to the next.
  • Adds a verbiage drift line to the primary-source panel.
  • With W-F also on, the timeline is anchored to structural terms, so you can see how that language changes over time.

How to read it: follow a term across eras. A term that appears, disappears or changes partner words is the drift.

Zero-Sum

  • Scans the text of a returned result for zero-sum narrative language: words that frame one group's gain as another's loss (for example "take from", "replace", "our jobs", "us vs them"), from overt wording through coded demographic-displacement wording.
  • The engine fetches the full text of the first result it can read (the primary source). If a site refuses, it moves down the list and shows the path it took.
  • When markers are found, shows Zero-Sum Narrative Detected with the source named (site, result number, words read), and each term with its inversion, its logic and its camouflage.
  • When none are found, it says so and names the source. When no result could be fetched, it says nothing was scanned.

How to read it: a match means the language is present in that source. It does not mean the source endorses it. A page that explains or criticises a zero-sum idea uses the same words. Open the page and read it in context.
